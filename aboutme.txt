I am a graduate student in the Department of Aerospace Engineering at Auburn University and a research assistant in the Vehicle Systems, Dynamics, and Design Laboratory. I joined Auburn in Fall 2022.

I am fascinated by the possibility of aircraft learning from flight on their own and improving their performance. My PhD research builds a foundation for this goal by exploring methods and tools for understanding aircraft behavior through flight data and using that knowledge to improve their models and control systems.

My research focuses on:

• Aircraft Model Development: Supporting rapid sizing and simulation of full-scale and subscale aircraft for Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) applications.
• Flight Testing and System Identification: Experimentally characterizing subscale aircraft for AAM applications centered on Simplified Vehicle Operations (SVO).
• Simulation Model Calibration and Validation: Using flight-test data to calibrate and validate simulation models.
• In-Flight Control Effector Fault Detection and Controller Reconfiguration: Developing methods to detect control effector failures and reconfigure controllers to protect flight within a constrained envelope.

After graduation, I hope to develop aircraft that learn from flight and improve their performance from one flight to the next, while communicating their performance limitations to both expert and non-expert users.

From 2019 to 2022, I worked as a drone design engineer at Prokura Innovations. With a small team of drone enthusiasts in Nepal, I designed, built, and tested a lift-plus-cruise vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) fixed-wing UAV for delivering emergency medicines to rural communities. This experience helped motivate my decision to pursue graduate study and my interest in learning from flight.

The world is ahead.”
